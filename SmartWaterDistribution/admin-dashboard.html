Allocation Rules Applied:
  1. Priority Order: Critical > High > Medium > Low > Very Low.
  2. Tie-Breaking: Requests within the same priority level are processed by arrival sequence.
  3. Network Reachability: Water is allocated ONLY if a working pipeline connects the area to an active reservoir.
  4. Reservoir Limits: Supply never exceeds remaining available reservoir volume.
  5. Partial Allocation: When supply is limited, available water is allocated partially, and the unmet shortfall is recorded.
  6. Pipeline Failure Handling: Disconnected areas receive 0 Litres and are flagged as Disconnected.

⚖️ Water Allocation Priority Levels

Water allocation follows a 5-tier priority hierarchy to protect essential services:

Stage 1: Critical — Multi-specialty hospitals, emergency healthcare facilities, ICU units. (Highest Priority)
Stage 2: High — Schools, colleges, and high-density residential community clusters.
Stage 3: Medium — Standard suburban residential colonies and township blocks.
Stage 4: Low — Low-density perimeter townships and non-essential commercial facilities.
Stage 5: Very Low — Public parks, ornamental fountains, and construction sites. (Lowest Priority)
